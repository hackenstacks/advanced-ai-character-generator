● I'm not a doctor, and a sharp pain on your right side can mean a lot of
  different things depending on exactly where it is — so I can't tell you what's
  causing yours. But here's a general orientation.

  Rough map of the right side:
  - Upper right, under the ribs — liver, gallbladder (gallstones), or a
  lung/pleura issue. Pain here after fatty meals, or with fever/yellowish skin,
  points toward gallbladder.
  - Right lower belly — the classic worry is appendicitis: pain that often
  starts near the navel then settles low-right, worsens over hours, with nausea,
  loss of appetite, or fever. This one is a medical emergency.
  - Flank / right back — kidney (stones or infection): often comes in waves, may
  go with painful urination, blood in urine, or fever.
  - Side wall / with movement or breathing — muscle strain or a rib/nerve issue,
  usually the most benign.
  - In women — ovarian or reproductive causes (cyst, ectopic pregnancy) can also
  present as right-sided pain.

  Get urgent / emergency care now if you have any of these:
  - Severe or rapidly worsening pain
  - Pain with fever, vomiting, or a rigid/very tender belly
  - Pain that's worse when you press then let go, or when you move/cough
  - Chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain spreading to shoulder/back
  - Blood in urine or stool, yellowing of eyes/skin
  - Fainting, or (if pregnant or possibly pregnant) any significant belly pain

  If it's mild, brief, and clearly tied to movement or a muscle, it's more
  likely something minor — but if it persists more than a day or two, keeps
  coming back, or you're unsure, please see a doctor. When something is
  genuinely severe or sudden, don't wait — call emergency services or go to the

  and intermittent rather than constant is generally reassuring, because organ
  pain (kidney, appendix, gallbladder) usually hurts regardless of how you move
  and tends to be constant, throbbing, or come in waves.

  Common culprits for what you're describing:
  - Muscle strain in the flank/oblique or the muscles along the spine — from a
  twist, lift, sleeping wrong, or even a hard cough.
  - Thoracolumbar / rib joint irritation — a facet joint or rib that pinches on
  rotation. Very often one-sided and movement-triggered.
  - Intercostal or a pinched nerve — can give a sharp catch with certain
  positions.

  Reasonable self-care for a few days:
  - Ease off the movements that trigger it; avoid heavy lifting/twisting.
  - Heat on the area (or ice early on if it feels inflamed).
  - Gentle stretching and staying mobile — don't fully immobilize it.
  - OTC pain relief (e.g. ibuprofen) if you tolerate it and have no reason to
  avoid it.

  But check in with a doctor if:
  - It doesn't improve within ~1–2 weeks, or keeps getting worse.
  - You get fever, painful/bloody urination, or nausea/vomiting (would point
  away from muscle, toward kidney).
  - The pain becomes constant, wakes you at night, or radiates around to the
  front/groin in waves.

  - There was a fall or trauma that could have cracked a rib.

  have the compositor's environment — WAYLAND_DISPLAY and XDG_RUNTIME_DIR. The
  clean way to guarantee that is to start the tmux session from inside the

  signals

● Here's how it works — I dug into fileSystemService.ts, ChatInterface.tsx,
  CommandApprovalModal.tsx, and HelpModal.tsx to be precise.

  How a character uses the Terminal

  1. Enable it per-character. In the Character editor there's a Terminal Access
  toggle (terminalEnabled). Off by default — a character can't touch the
  terminal until you flip it on.

  2. The character emits a command in its reply. The app scans each response for
  three trigger patterns (CommandApprovalModal.tsx):
  - [command] — square brackets
  - a fenced code block: ```bash, ```sh, ```shell, or ```terminal
  - /execute <command> or /run <command>

  3. The app runs it and feeds the result back. ChatInterface extracts the
  command, runs fileSystemService.executeCommand(...), posts a system line —
  🖥️ Terminal (CharacterName): $ <cmd>\n<output> — then sends [Terminal Output]:
  … back into the model so the character can react to what it "saw." There's an
  approval modal (and a 🚫 Terminal Denied path) so you can gate commands
  before they run.

  What the "terminal" actually is (important)

  It's a sandboxed, in-app virtual shell — not your real OS. executeCommand
  implements fake pwd / ls / cd / cat / touch / mkdir / rm / echo against an
  in-memory filesystem (createDefaultFileSystem). So a character rm-ing
  something only touches the sandbox, never your disk.

  Beyond the fake FS, it has real bridges (in fileSystemService.ts):

  ┌────────────────┬────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┐
  │   Capability   │        Function        │            Reaches            │

  │                │                        │ languages)                    │
